## 8. Limitations

1. **E0 only.** Nothing here is runtime evidence; E1–E4 are `NOT_RUN` across all eight packages by their own declarations. A validated schema does not prove an adapter refuses a tool call; a documented restore drill does not prove a restore.
2. **No OpenAPI validator.** As stated in §3, my PC05 verdict rests on structural and cross-reference checks, not on spec compliance. A conformance run remains outstanding and should not be assumed from this PASS.
3. **Semantic-layer checks were read, not executed.** PC06's `reject_semantic` fixtures are correct in declaring that the schema accepts them; whether the downstream citation-resolution and canary checks actually reject them is untestable without code, and both are `NOT_RUN`.
4. **Prose contracts were read for the specific properties the packet named**, not audited line by line — `secrets.md`, `internet-boundary.md`, `backup-restore.md`, `collector-probe.md`, `cli-acp-probe.md` and `grounding.md` total well over 100 KB and I checked their required elements, headers, oracles and cross-references rather than every claim.
5. **My field-level checker walks any list keyed by an entity name.** That is broader than some package checkers and is how `F-A1R3-01` surfaced; it may also over-report where a fixture legitimately nests an unrelated object under an entity-shaped key. I inspected the 89 hits by name and all six distinct names are annotation-style, which is why I scoped the finding to gate trustworthiness rather than to contract correctness.
6. **Concurrency with PC09/PC10.** Both were being written during this audit. No PC09/PC10 path appears in the manifest and the snapshot was byte-identical at start and end, so they did not perturb the candidate — but several findings (`F-A1R3-01`, `-02`, `-03`) name obligations PC09 will inherit, and I say nothing about how it discharges them.
